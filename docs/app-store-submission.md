## Description — en-US

```
Know how long your home battery will last before the power comes back.

Battery ETA reads your Marstek Venus E 3.0 over your home Wi‑Fi and turns the numbers into one answer: the time left at the current load. While the battery charges, it shows the time until full.

TIME LEFT
• EMPTY IN: time until the battery reaches your reserve
• FULL IN: time until it is fully charged
• IF OUTAGE: how long the backup socket would run if the grid went down now
• Charge in percent and power in watts

POWER BACK AT
• Enter when the grid returns and get a plain answer: "Lasts until 18:00, 1 h 20 min to spare" or "Runs out at 16:40, 40 min short"
• In Kyiv, pick your address once and the time fills in from the YASNO outage schedule

WIDGETS
• Home Screen: charge ring, or charge with time left
• Lock Screen: round gauge or a line with time left
• See the numbers during an outage without opening the app

TODAY
• A chart of today's charge and hourly power
• Energy charged and used, and time on battery

ALERTS
• A notification when less than 30 minutes are left, and when the battery reaches the reserve

SET UP IN A MINUTE
• Turn on Local API in the Marstek app
• Tap Find battery, and the app finds it on your Wi‑Fi
• No account and no sign-in

PRIVACY
Battery ETA only reads the battery status and never changes a battery setting. Readings stay on your device. No ads, no analytics, no tracking.

REQUIREMENTS
• Marstek Venus E 3.0 with firmware V150 or newer and Local API turned on
• iPhone or iPad on the same Wi‑Fi as the battery
• Away from home, widgets show the last reading

Battery ETA is an independent app. It is not made by, endorsed by or affiliated with Marstek.
```

## App Privacy

- Data collection: **No, we do not collect data from this app.**
- Why: readings and settings stay in the app's storage. The street name typed on the Address screen goes to the public YASNO API to look up the outage group, and Use my location reverse-geocodes once through Apple. Neither the developer nor any SDK in the app keeps that data.

Notes:

```
Battery ETA reads a Marstek Venus E 3.0 home battery over the local Wi‑Fi network (UDP to the battery's IP address, port 30000). It needs the physical battery on the same network, so without one the app shows "NO LINK" after "Save and check". There is no login and no server.

The attached video shows on an iPhone with the battery:
1. Find battery, then allowing Local Network access when iOS asks.
2. The live reading: charge, power and EMPTY IN.
3. Power back at: the LASTS or RUNS OUT verdict.
4. Adding the Home Screen and Lock Screen widgets.
5. Denying Local Network: the widget shows ALLOW LAN and tapping it opens Settings.

The app only reads status (the battery's ES.GetStatus method) and never changes battery settings. The Address screen looks up the user's outage group from the public YASNO schedule API for Kyiv; location is used once, only when the user taps "Use my location".
```

## Demo video script (about 90 s)

1. Start a screen recording from Control Center with the app closed.
2. Open Battery ETA, tap Find battery, tap Allow on the Local Network prompt, pick the battery.
3. Hold 5 s on the Now card while a load runs, so EMPTY IN shows a time.
4. Set Power back at to an hour from now and show the verdict.
5. Go home, add the medium widget, then the Lock Screen rectangular widget.
6. Settings › Privacy & Security › Local Network: turn Battery ETA off, tap ALLOW LAN on the widget to show it opens Settings, turn it back on.
